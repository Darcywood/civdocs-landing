# Competitor Research: CivDocs vs Record TIME

> **Purpose:** Source material for `/compare/civdocs-vs-record-time`. Research only. Frame: honest/balanced, slight CivDocs lean. **Key insight: Record TIME is a digital docketing / paperless-forms tool, narrower in scope than CivDocs.** It's a strong, affordable, easy-to-adopt tool for replacing paper dockets — but it's not a full operational platform with job costing, invoicing integrations, and plant-hire-specific workflows. The honest wedge: *Record TIME digitises your paperwork; CivDocs runs your civil business.*

---

## 1. Company snapshot

- **Product:** Record TIME — digital docketing & paperless job-management system. "Replace your paper systems… spend less time chasing paper docket approvals."
- **Origin:** Australian. Record TIME Pty Ltd, ABN 99 604 582 649, based in Fyshwick ACT. Operating since ~2015.
- **Scale:** Small/SMB vendor. Modest social footprint (LinkedIn ~61 followers, FB ~392 likes, IG ~331 followers — i.e. a smaller operation than Varicon/Assignar). Active at AU construction expos (Diesel Dirt & Turf).
- **Category:** Digital dockets + customisable forms + job scheduling. No-code "replicate your paper docket" engine. Android/iOS + web backend.
- **Positioning:** "Built for hands, not spreadsheets." 14-day go-paperless guarantee, 3-step transition (paper form → upload → go live). Free trial available ("Try Record TIME Free").

## 2. What Record TIME does (feature inventory)

- **Digital docket book / custom forms engine** — flagship. Replicate any paper docket: earthmoving dockets, delivery dockets, daily construction timesheets, pre-start checklists, SWMS, JSAs, risk assessments, chemical hazard forms, property inspections. Fully customisable fields, rules, validations.
- **Job scheduling** — schedule employees & plant; assign who/where/with what; on-the-fly changes; resource utilisation tracking.
- **Timesheets** — daily construction timesheet as a docket type; job timers.
- **Pre-start checklists / safety forms** — templating engine for compliance docs.
- **QR codes** — for dockets / sign-ins.
- **Proof of delivery / proof of work** — digital signatures, timestamps.
- **Invoicing** — described as a "digital docketing and invoice system"; turn dockets into invoices.
- **Backend web portal** — search dockets, view business activity, manage data, collaboration (multiple users on same docket).
- **Industry reach** — markets to construction, earthmoving, crane & rigging, transport, healthcare (cross-industry, not civil-exclusive).
- **Integration:** Xero integration listed (docketing → accounting). Less emphasis on MYOB / deep accounting workflows than CivDocs/Varicon.

## 3. ICP — who Record TIME is really for

- Trades, earthmoving, transport, civil **operators whose primary pain is paper** — chasing dockets, signatures, delivery proof.
- Small businesses wanting a fast, cheap, low-friction way to go paperless.
- Cross-industry (not civil-specific) — flexibility is the selling point, but it means it's not purpose-built around civil workflows the way CivDocs or Varicon are.

**Overlap with CivDocs:** Partial. Both do digital forms/dockets, timesheets, pre-starts, scheduling. But Record TIME is **docket/forms-first and general-purpose**; CivDocs is **civil-specific and operations-complete** (job costing, machine-hour costing, plant hire logbooks, invoicing, travel allowance, material delivery tracking, deeper accounting).

## 5. Strengths (be honest)

- **Excellent, flexible forms/docket engine.** No-code replication of any paper form is genuinely strong and a real reason people pick it.
- **Fast, low-friction adoption** — 14-day go-live, free trial, simple UI "built for hands."
- **Affordable / SMB-friendly** — accessible for small operators replacing paper.
- **Australian, long-running** (since 2015), expo presence, real customer base in earthmoving/transport.
- **Cross-industry flexibility** — works beyond civil.
- Covers the core paper-replacement jobs (dockets, timesheets, pre-starts, POD) well.

## 6. Weaknesses / gaps (where CivDocs can win)

- **Narrower scope.** Forms/docket-first. Lacks CivDocs' depth in **job costing, machine-hour costing, plant hire logbooks, material delivery tracking, travel allowance, deeper invoicing/accounting workflows.**
- **General-purpose, not civil-native.** Markets across healthcare/transport/etc.; not architected specifically around civil-contractor and plant-hire workflows (MGA coordinates, machine control, civil job costing, etc.).
- **Lighter accounting integration** — Xero listed, but not the dual Xero+MYOB + job-costing-sync depth CivDocs targets.
- **Smaller vendor footprint** — though this cuts both ways (CivDocs is also founder-led/small).
- Not positioned around **plant hire** as a first-class vertical.

## 7. CivDocs differentiation angles (for the page)

1. **Scope: paperwork tool vs operational platform.** Record TIME replaces paper dockets/forms. CivDocs does that *and* runs job costing, invoicing, plant hire logbooks, machine-hour costing, scheduling, material delivery — the whole operational stack for a civil business.
2. **Civil-native vs general-purpose.** CivDocs is built specifically for civil contractors and plant hire (workflows, terminology, costing). Record TIME is a flexible cross-industry forms tool.
3. **Plant hire logbooks + machine-hour costing** — first-class in CivDocs; not Record TIME's focus.
4. **Deeper financial workflows** — Xero + MYOB, job costing, invoicing tied to actual civil job structures.
5. **One system vs forms layer + separate tools.** A contractor on Record TIME may still need separate job-costing/invoicing tooling; CivDocs consolidates.

**Keep it fair:** Record TIME's forms engine and ease of adoption are real strengths — acknowledge them. Don't claim it can't do timesheets or invoicing (it does, at a docket level). The honest frame: *"If your main problem is paper dockets and you want a flexible digital forms tool, Record TIME does that well. If you're a civil contractor or plant hire business that needs job costing, plant logbooks, machine-hour costing and invoicing built around how civil work actually runs — not just digital paperwork — CivDocs is purpose-built for that."*
