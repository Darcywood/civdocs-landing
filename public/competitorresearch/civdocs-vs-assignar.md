# Competitor Research: CivDocs vs Assignar

> **Purpose:** Source material for `/compare/civdocs-vs-assignar`. Research only. Frame: honest/balanced, slight CivDocs lean. Assignar is a serious, well-funded, civil-native competitor — bigger and more enterprise than CivDocs. Don't pretend it's weak. The winning angle is **simplicity, price, and self-serve fit for small-to-mid civil contractors** vs Assignar's heavier, enterprise-leaning, sales-gated platform.

---

## 1. Company snapshot

- **Product:** Assignar — cloud operations platform for construction & civil subcontractors.
- **Origin:** Australian-founded; now international (strong US presence too). Among the most "construction-native" platforms in the AU market.
- **Scale:** "800+ contractors / 800+ customers." Well-established, venture-backed, enterprise sales motion.
- **Positioning:** "Connects field to finance" — one platform for crews, compliance, and cash flow. Recently leaning into T&M-to-invoice automation, job costing, and margin protection.
- **Target verticals (their words):** asphalt, concrete, drilling, piling, excavation, earthworks, NDD, rail, scaffolding, crane, demolition, traffic management. **Heavily civil/self-perform subcontractor focused.**

## 2. What Assignar does (feature inventory)

- **Scheduling / resource allocation** — drag-and-drop qualified+available workers and equipment into a calendar; instant notify; strong for civil resource allocation and shift tracking. This is a core strength.
- **Timesheets** — mobile clock in/out, crew timesheets, supervisor-completed timesheets, geo-located submissions, meal/travel allowances, breaks, dockets, customised pay rates per activity.
- **Custom forms / inspections / compliance** — SWMS, pre-starts, machine pre-starts, safety inspections, leave forms; mobile submission.
- **Compliance management** — license/competency expiry alerts, induction tracking. (Note: a competitor review claims Assignar does NOT link crew scheduling to licence validation as tightly as some rivals — there's a manual compliance-check step.)
- **Dockets** — real-time docket capture.
- **Job costing / T&M to invoice** — newer push: link verified field work to billing, cost codes applied through the day, daily cost reporting.
- **Insights / reporting** — analytics module.
- **Integrations** — Xero (payroll/accounting), **Procore** (positions Assignar as a resource-scheduling layer inside a Procore stack). Integration-partner model for syncing field data to ERP/accounting.

## 3. ICP — who Assignar is really for

- Construction **subcontractors and self-perform GCs** with significant crew + plant to coordinate.
- Mid-sized firms and up. Strong fit for firms that already run **Procore or Xero** and want a scheduling/field-ops layer.
- Civil-heavy: earthworks, infrastructure, traffic management, etc.
- Described by a third party as "a practical choice for mid-sized firms that need scheduling and compliance documentation without a large implementation effort" — i.e. lighter than enterprise EHS but still aimed up-market relative to a solo-operator tool.

**Overlap with CivDocs:** Real but partial. Both serve civil contractors with timesheets, forms, scheduling, dockets. Assignar skews **larger / more enterprise / more crew-coordination-heavy**. CivDocs skews **smaller civil contractors / plant hire / all-in-one operational simplicity**.

## 4. Pricing — a key battleground

- **No public pricing.** Demo/sales-gated. Annual contract model.
- **No free trial** — and this is explicitly a sore point in reviews. One Capterra reviewer: *"No free trial, had to sign up full amount."*
- A switcher review (moved to Dashpivot/Sitemate) claimed the alternative was *"99% less"* cost and that they rebuilt forms themselves "at a 3rd of the price." Treat as one customer's experience, but it signals Assignar is perceived as **expensive** for smaller operators.
- Annual fee paid upfront; reviewer complaint that "once annual fee was paid we never got the support promised."

**Angle for CivDocs:** transparent pricing + free trial + no big upfront annual commitment = lower-risk entry for a small civil contractor. This is one of the strongest, most concrete wedges in the whole comparison set.

## 5. Strengths (be honest)

- **Genuinely civil/construction-native**, Australian-founded, deep in the exact verticals CivDocs targets.
- **Best-in-class scheduling / resource allocation** for crews + equipment. Mature, well-reviewed module.
- **Strong compliance/forms tooling** — license expiry alerts, inductions, SWMS, real-time docket and timesheet capture.
- **Established, 800+ customers, real enterprise credibility**, Procore integration.
- Good reviews on customer success managers and reducing paper workload; Insights reporting praised for cutting admin hours.
- Geo-located timesheets, per-activity pay rates — sophisticated field-data capture.

## 6. Weaknesses / gaps (where CivDocs can win)

Drawn from real reviews — usable, but attribute as user feedback, don't overstate:
- **App glitches / reliability complaints.** Multiple reviewers cite app bugs, timesheet issues, and "constant complaints daily from employees… so many glitches." One tech-savvy team still struggled.
- **Admin-side scheduling hard on mobile.** A reviewer: "near impossible to schedule works… from an app" on the admin side.
- **No free trial; full upfront annual commitment.** Friction + risk for small contractors.
- **Perceived expensive** vs lighter alternatives (the "1/3 of the price" switcher).
- **Post-sale support gap** alleged by at least one reviewer ("never got the support promised").
- **Couldn't view site plans/paperwork from app on site** (one review).
- **Heavier implementation / enterprise orientation** — may be more platform than a small civil or plant-hire business needs.
- Compliance linking weaker than some rivals (manual licence-check step per the Kynection comparison).

## 7. CivDocs differentiation angles (for the page)

1. **Right-sized for small-to-mid civil + plant hire.** CivDocs isn't trying to be an enterprise crew-ops platform — it's the operational system a 5–50 person civil/plant-hire business actually needs, without the implementation weight.
2. **Free trial + transparent tiered pricing vs sales-gated annual contract.** Try before you buy; know the price; no big upfront lock-in.
3. **All-in-one operational coverage at the SMB level** — timesheets, pre-starts, plant hire logbooks, invoicing, job costing, scheduling, material delivery, machine-hour costing, Xero/MYOB.
4. **Plant hire logbooks + machine-hour costing** as first-class features (Assignar is crew/field-ops first; plant-hire-specific logbook workflows are less central).
5. **Simplicity / reliability framing** — position CivDocs as the simpler, cleaner day-to-day tool. (Use carefully; lean on "designed to be simple for site crews" rather than directly trashing Assignar's app.)
